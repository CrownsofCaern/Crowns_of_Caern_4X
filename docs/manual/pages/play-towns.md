## The town screen

Open a town with `T` (or click it). The painting shows what is built. Below it are the buildings you can raise, the dwellings and their creatures, the garrison and the hero who is visiting. `Tab` and the arrows switch between your towns.

## Building

Each people has about thirty buildings in five groups:

- **Civic:** the hall chain that raises your daily gold, and the treasury.
- **Fortifications:** walls that defend the town and make every dwelling grow faster.
- **Dwellings:** one per creature tier. A new dwelling starts with one week of creatures.
- **Upgrades:** one per creature, letting you recruit its upgraded form and convert stacks you already have.
- **Specials:** each people has its own, such as the Masons' Lodge, which makes later buildings cheaper in timber and ore, or the Trophy Pit, which pays gold for every victory.

You may raise **one building per town per day**. A month of income pays for well under half of a town, so every choice closes another.

::: tip "Walls or dwellings?"
Walls protect the town and add a quarter to every dwelling's growth. A second dwelling adds a new kind of creature. Early peoples (the Host, the Frostmarch) gain most from dwellings; the Forge gains more from walls.
:::

## Recruiting

Dwellings fill on the first day of each week, and what you do not recruit stays for later. Recruit with a hero standing in the town and the creatures join the hero. Without a hero, they join the garrison and defend the town.

An army holds up to ten stacks. Move stacks between a hero and the garrison by picking a stack and then a slot. `Split` or Shift+click moves part of a stack.

## Upgrades

Each creature has one upgraded form. Once the upgrade building stands, the dwelling offers the upgraded creature from the same weekly pool, and you can convert whole stacks for the difference in price. Upgrades add a little at tier 1 and a great deal at the top: see each people's roster for what each upgrade adds.

## Tavern

The tavern hires another hero (2500 gold) with a small army of its own. You may lead up to eight. Tavern heroes come without equipment.

## Market

The market trades any resource for any other at a poor rate. Each further market you own improves it, up to a limit. Trading there and back always loses value.

## Ultimate buildings

Every people has one very expensive last building. It needs the tier 8 dwelling and the treasury, costs 30,000 gold and rare resources, and upgrades every creature of that people in all your armies.

| People | Building | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Aldmere Concord | Reliquary of Holy Arms | +2 attack, +1 morale, blows ignore 20% of defense |
| Ashen Host | Brand-Pit of the Abyss | +10% damage; wounds burn for 15% more next round |
| Hollow Dominion | The Undying Choir | +10% hit points; drains 20% of damage dealt |
| Thornwood Covenant | Heart of the Mother Tree | +2 defense, +15% ranged damage |
| Sunken Forge | The Deep King's Anvil | +2 defense, +10% hit points, -10% damage from shots |
| Frostmarch Clans | Hall of the Endless Feast | +2 attack, +1 initiative, damage builds each round, morale never below 0 |

## Sieges

A town with walls fights back. A hero standing in their own town defends together with the garrison, and the walls add to their defense. A town with a hero in it counts as guarded even with an empty garrison.
