## Days and weeks

The game is played in days. On each day every player moves their heroes and acts in their towns, then ends the day. Seven days make a week; at the start of each week every dwelling adds its growth.

Income arrives every morning: each town pays according to its hall, each mine pays its resource.

## Movement and terrain

A hero's movement depends on the slowest creature in the army, the hero's skills and boots, and the ground.

| Ground | Movement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Road | cheapest | Roads link towns. Keep to them when you can. |
| Grassland, dirt | normal | The Concord's native ground. |
| Forest | slow | Native to the Thornwood. |
| Rocky highland | slow | Native to the Forge and the Umbral Court. |
| Swamp | slow | Native to the Hollow Dominion and the Abyssal Shoal. |
| Snowfield | slow | Native to the Frostmarch. Cold for some peoples (see below). |
| Cinder wastes | slow | Native to the Ashen Host. |

Every people fights better on its **native ground** (+1 attack for every creature) and worse on its **hostile ground** (-1 attack). Some creatures have their own affinities on top: a bog lurker in the swamp is a different animal.

::: warning "The cold"
In campaigns, an army standing on snow whose people cannot bear the cold loses a tenth of every such stack each morning. Heroes in towns are safe, and so is an army led by a hero with the Fieldcraft skill, the Frostcaller's Rime Shroud, or two pieces of the Forgewarm Harness.
:::

## Resources and mines

| Resource | Used for |
|---|---|
| Gold | Creatures, buildings, heroes. Everything. |
| Timber | Buildings, especially early ones. |
| Ore | Buildings, walls, and the Forge's creatures. |
| Quicksilver | The Ashen Host, the Hollow Dominion and the Forge. |
| Brimstone | The Ashen Host and the Forge. |
| Crystal | The Concord, the Forge, the Frostmarch, the Umbral Court, the Shoal. |
| Gems | The Concord, the Hollow, the Thornwood, the Umbral Court, the Shoal. |

Each people's page lists the two rare resources it relies on. Hold those mines and your top tiers come early; lose them and they never come at all.

## Things on the map

- **Towns** belong to a player or stand neutral. A neutral town with a garrison has to be fought for.
- **Mines** pay every day to whoever holds them. An enemy hero can take them back by walking onto them.
- **Guards** are wild creatures. They do not move, and they block the tile they stand on.
- **Treasure:** resource piles, chests, and armour and weapons for your heroes.
- **Encounters** are small scenes: a shrine, a caravan, a stranger on the road. They offer a choice.
- **Traders** on the roads sell equipment.
- **Cobolt trading posts** belong to no one. See below.

### Landmarks

Rare buildings give lasting help. Some are guarded and must be won first.

| Landmark | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Outcast Camp | Recruit a tier 1-3 creature of another people, or a wild one. Refills weekly. |
| Pyre Citadel | Recruit a tier 5-6 creature of one of the peoples. |
| Sellsword Gate | A new tier 2-4 company each week; last week's is gone. |
| Oathstone | +1 defense, once for each hero. |
| Bleeding Rift | +1 attack and +1 power, once for each hero. |
| Pilgrims' Rest | +1 morale and more of the fallen recovered, for seven days. |
| Wardens' Causeway | +20% movement and +1 vision, for seven days. |
| Elder Rings | 1200 experience, once for each hero. |

Dwelling landmarks are captured by stepping onto them; your hero recruits there by standing on them.

### Cobolt trading posts

Cobolts are small, tough traders who never take sides. They keep fortified trading houses in the wild, and every post sells three things: one kind of cobolt recruit, one artefact, and one resource exchange about 40% cheaper than a market. Walking onto a post opens the shop peacefully. Recruits and the exchange restock each week up to a cap, but the artefact is sold only once.

A post never shows its guard. Your scouts can watch its patrols, but nobody learns how many cobolts wait in the vaults or what the odds would be. You can choose to **rob** a post. The moment you commit, that post's cobolts turn hostile to you for good, even if you lose or withdraw. Win, and you take a fixed cache of gold and the unsold artefact, and the post is a ruin from then on, closed to everyone.

Random maps place one to three posts in the neutral lands, and every hand-made map has at least one. Computer players buy recruits at posts, but only from the second week on, and never try to rob them.

## Omens and fortune

From the second week on, each new week brings an **omen** that holds for everyone: a good week for growth, a lean week for gold, a week when the wild armies are stronger, or, most often, a quiet one. The omen is shown beside the date.

Each day each player may also meet a **fortune** of their own: a windfall, a fire in the stores, deserters who want to join.

## Scouting

Point at a hostile army to see roughly how big it is and how a fight would go. To know exactly, press `S` to **send a scout**. It costs some movement and the target must be close. For the rest of the day you see exact numbers and your exact odds.

On the **Hard odds** setting the game never shows win chances. Scouts still count the enemy.

## Winning and losing

A player with no heroes and no towns is out. A player who loses every town has seven days to take one back. In a standard game the last player or team standing wins. Scenario maps and campaign chapters may set other goals; open **Objectives** to read them.
